Field notes

What I kept seeing.

Watching how teams actually do strategic work, the same nine patterns kept showing up.

01
Fragmented docs.

Spread across people, drives, decks, threads. The most useful slide is on someone else's laptop.

02
No real source of truth.

Five docs cite five different numbers. Nobody knows which one we actually believe.

03
Everyone has a different view.

Each person carries their own private version of "what we've decided." No reconciliation happens.

04
Stale info scattered everywhere.

Last year's numbers, last quarter's roadmap, last cycle's deadlines — still flying around in this year's decks.

05
No shared lens for opportunity.

Hunting new bets, everyone optimizes in a different direction — no shared rubric about who we are, what fits, what doesn't.

06
Expertise stays siloed.

VPs know the company. PMs know the market. Engineers know what's buildable. Nobody assembles the picture.

07
Brainstorms die in long docs.

Each person prompts ChatGPT alone, dumps a four-page doc nobody else reads. We'd be better served by a collaborative session where everyone guides the LLM together, reviews and refines on top of shared rules of the game.

08
Everyone imagines a different future.

Five people in a room, five private pictures of what happens next. The board sees one upside, engineers see one downside, the customer team sees something else entirely — and none of these futures ever get reconciled into a shared one the whole team can act on.

09
High-potential ideas vanish in the chaos.

The next big bet gets mentioned in passing on a Tuesday standup, captured by nobody, forgotten by Friday. The ideas that survive in our org are the ones loud enough to fight through the noise — not necessarily the right ones.

These aren't different problems. They're symptoms of one missing thing.

Highlight 6 / 7 · Self-wiring graph

A graph that wires itself.

Every ingest runs a deterministic, zero-LLM-call post-pass that tags entities (Person · Company · Organization · Product) and adds role-typed edges the LLM doesn't reliably surface. Ask questions vector search can't reach.

📚 you ask
Who works at Acme AI? What did Bob invest in this quarter?
🔗 typed-edge query
GET /api/entities/relations?relation=works_at&target_label=Acme
Jane Doe — works_at → Acme AI
Bob Smith — works_at → Acme AI
Carol Lee — advises → Acme AI
📐 brain answers in prose
TL;DR: Three people are wired to Acme AI: Jane (works), Bob (works), Carol (advises). Bob also worked at Globex Corp (team-roster.md)…
🏷 Deterministic typing

Person · Company · Organization · Product tagged by rule, not by Claude. Runs on every rebuild. Zero API cost.

🔗 Role-typed edges

works_at · founded · invested_in · attended · advises. Regex-pattern matched from any markdown / text in the workspace.

🎯 Reaches what vectors can't

"All advisors of Beta Holdings" · "Everyone who attended Stanford" · "All investments led by Bob in Q2". Structural queries, not similarity guesses.

🧬 Composes with everything

Refine KB can override a wrong type. Audit can flag stale relations. Gap-analysis still surfaces what's missing.

Honest scope: the deterministic pass runs on top of Claude extraction, not instead of it; role-edge matches resolve against entities Claude already pulled out, so precision is conservative by default.

Highlight 7 / 7 · Isolation

One app. Many brains.

Workspaces are first-class — each team gets its own corpus, graph, conversations, memory, rubrics, intents, playbooks, artifacts, and ForeSight sessions. Strict isolation. Optional templated copy from a sibling workspace on creation.

🗂 Strict scope

Every API call carries an X-Workspace-Id header. No cross-bleed. Ever.

🌱 Seed from another

New workspace can copy rubrics + intents from a sibling — snapshots, not live links. Edits stay local.

🧰 Per-workspace tooling

Intents, rubrics, playbooks, personas all overridable per workspace. Built-ins always restorable.

📈 Diff-aware ingest

Every rebuild emits a what changed diff — added / removed / relabeled nodes — surfaced in Refine KB.

Replaces: a single shared drive nobody can prune without breaking five teams.
