Onboarding Guide · Q2 2026

Make the brain
yours in five minutes.

InnoBrain is your team's strategy brain — a workspace where your documents, your team's experience, and your simulated futures compound into reusable decisions. This guide walks you through the gestures that matter, by role and by surface.

⏱ 5-minute quickstart 🧑‍💼 VP · Director · PM · EM · SE 📍 localhost:8080
First five minutes

The Quickstart.

Four steps. Each takes under a minute. By the end you'll have run a Playbook against your own data and seen InnoBrain's value first-hand.

First visit? A welcome modal greets you on your first browser session and points you to create your workspace. You can revisit it any time via DevTools (localStorage.removeItem('innobrain.user_initialized')) or skip it with Esc. After that, a floating Quickstart checklist in the bottom-right tracks your progress through these four steps and pulses the target UI for whichever step is next.
01

Create a workspace

Navigate to localhost:8080. Click the WORKSPACE button at the top (now visibly accent-tinted) and create one for your team's domain — e.g. "Marketplace Strategy 2026". Workspaces are isolated brains; Sales' brain shouldn't mix with Engineering's.

The Quickstart checklist will pulse the workspace button for you.
02

Add your first documents

The left sidebar's 📤 Upload files dropzone is the primary action. Drag in 2-5 of your most load-bearing docs — PDFs, decks, board memos. The graph builds in ~60 seconds per PDF. You can also 🔗 Add by URL, 💻 Add code repository, or 🌐 Research the web from the same sidebar.

Five high-signal docs beats twenty mediocre ones. The dropzone pulses while it's your current step.
03

Run your first Playbook

Open the Playbooks tab (the checklist will highlight it) and pick Find unexplored ideas. The kickoff form shows three real scenarios — two grounded in your KB, one wildcard. Click the most interesting one. Hit Run. ~5 minutes later you'll have a typed Opportunity Scan. This is the aha moment.

Your global Rubric + Memory are auto-applied. If the suggestions don't land, hit ↻ Refresh for a fresh set.
04

Refine what the brain got wrong

Open the Refine KB tab → Facts in the KB. Scan for anything stale or wrong. Click ✎ Edit, fix it, save. The LLM cites your version — with your attribution — from then on. Code-derived facts (functions, classes) are hidden by default since they don't fit the refinement gesture; toggle Show code nodes if you need them.

First-week target: 5-10 refinements. After that, the brain starts feeling like yours.
That's it. You've now created your own workspace, ingested your docs, run a real strategic analysis with your team's framing already applied, and shaped the brain with your own experience. Everything from here is variation on these four gestures.
📐 Rubric and 🧠 Memory are global. The Appfire Context rubric and the persistent memory store are shared across every workspace you create — no per-workspace setup needed. Edit them once from the header buttons, and every workspace inherits the change. Tune your Rubric when your company strategy shifts; add to Memory when context changes.
Find your path

By your role.

Different lenses, different gestures. Pick yours and use it as your default mode.

VP

Stress-test bets before committing.

You make 5-10 high-leverage decisions a quarter. Your job: kill the bad ones before they kill you.

  • Run ForeSight before any major commitment
  • Pressure-test strategy playbook for board-level bets
  • Use Build/Buy/Partner when capital allocation is on the table
→ Default tab: Playbooks
DIR

Keep the brain fresh + aligned.

You're the connective tissue. The KB has to stay current and the framing has to stay coherent or your team will diverge.

  • Run Audit KB freshness weekly
  • Maintain the Rubric as the company evolves
  • Promote useful Conversation insights via Save as KB fact
→ Default tab: Refine KB
PM

Turn customer signal into shipped decisions.

You live between buyer pain and engineering reality. Your job: connect them with evidence.

  • Draft a PRD playbook for v1 specs
  • Plan a launch (GTM) for positioning + channels
  • Save every customer insight as a KB fact
→ Default tab: Playbooks → Draft a PRD
EM

Inject buildable reality into every plan.

Strategy meetings often miss what's actually shippable. Your job: ground the plan in real engineering.

  • Codebase health playbook for technical audits
  • Pre-mortem on a plan before sprint kickoff
  • Add engineering-reality dissents on overoptimistic claims
→ Default tab: Playbooks → Codebase health
SE

Spot what's wrong. Make it right.

You see the actual edges of the codebase, the unspoken constraints, the things "everyone knows" that nobody wrote down. Capture them.

  • Use Conversations as a thinking partner — durable threads
  • Hit 💡 save as KB fact whenever you correct an answer
  • Run Find unexplored ideas for technical opportunity scans
→ Default tab: Conversations
Each surface, what it's for

The tabs, one at a time.

Each tab does one job well. Pick the right one and you'll cut your time in half.

💬

Conversations

Durable research threads, multi-turn, graph-grounded.

Where you think with the brain. Each conversation is a thread you can revisit. Every answer is grounded in the graph + your refinements + your rubric.

  • New conversation → pick an Intent (Explore, Evaluate, Red-team, etc.) and a Rubric in the top bar.
  • Pin key answers and key nodes so they survive across turns.
  • 💡 Save as KB fact on any answer captures it as a refinement with one click.
  • Export report creates a clean markdown summary for sharing.
When to use You're exploring, learning, or stress-testing. Not when you need a typed artifact — for that, use a Playbook.
📘

Playbooks

Multi-step workflows that emit a typed artifact.

Where you produce a brief. A Playbook is a fixed chain of LLM steps that always ends in a typed artifact (Opportunity Scan, Strategy Brief, PRD Draft, Launch Plan, Codebase Audit, KB Health Report).

  • Kickoff form shows three suggested scenarios from your KB + one wildcard. Click to fill, edit if you want.
  • Web grounding is on by default — turn off for sensitive runs.
  • Fact-check adds a verification step at the cost of one extra LLM round.
  • Comments & refinement — every artifact is reviewable. Add a comment per section, click ↻ Refine with comments for a new version.
  • Compare versions — every refinement creates a new version with a diff view.
When to use You need an output that survives the meeting — something to share, version, and reference later.
🔮

ForeSight

Multi-persona simulation across five horizons.

Where you see the future before it happens. ForeSight runs a multi-round debate across up to 12 personas (Bull, Bear, Customer, Competitor, Regulator, Investor, …) at the horizon you choose.

  • Pick personas — usually 4 to 7. More than 7 dilutes the signal.
  • Pick a horizon — 3mo for tactical, 1y for go-to-market, 3y+ for category bets.
  • Multi-round — Round 2+ personas see Round 1. Positions evolve. Convergence AND disagreement get surfaced.
  • Output saves as a Strategy Brief artifact you can revisit.
When to use Before any commitment your team can't easily reverse — a hire, a bet, a launch, an acquisition.
🧬

Refine KB

Edit the present truth. Restore the original any time.

Where you shape the brain. Two sub-tabs:

  • Facts in the KB — every fact the brain currently believes. The view the LLM sees when it answers. Click ✎ Edit on any row to fix it. Filter by topic, source file, "edited only", or "stale ≥6 months".
  • My refinements — your edits history. Filter by kind (Fix · Add · Confirm · Doubt) or search.

Four refinement kinds:

  • Fix — replace what the KB got wrong
  • Add — inject facts the corpus is silent on
  • Confirm — vouch for a fact you've verified
  • Doubt — flag a claim without overriding
When to use Any time you spot something wrong, missing, or stale. The cheapest gesture in the app — under 30 seconds — and it compounds for the whole team.
📐

Rubrics & Memory

Global rules of the game, applied to every answer in every workspace.

Both live in the app header — visible from every tab — and both are global across all workspaces. They power every LLM call across Conversations, Playbooks, ForeSight, Ask Graph, and Explain Node. No per-workspace setup needed.

  • 📐 Rubrics — company-specific framing rules. The default Appfire Context rubric covers capital constraints, anti-Sherlocking tests, distribution leverage, regulatory tailwinds, audit honesty, decisiveness. Edit it once; the LLM's whole personality shifts across every workspace.
  • 🧠 Memory — durable user-asserted facts that flavor every answer. "We're in a hiring freeze." "Our Q3 priority is renewals." "Filip is the source of truth on Marketplace."
When to use Set Rubrics once per quarter. Add Memory entries when context shifts — and every workspace absorbs the new reality immediately.
Common recipes

What good usage looks like.

Stop staring at a blank box. These are the patterns we've seen power-users settle into.

🗓 Pre-meeting prep

Two hours before any strategy meeting:

  • Run Pressure-test strategy with the meeting's central question
  • Skim the Strategy Brief; pull the convergent + divergent claims
  • Walk into the meeting with the meeting's worst objections already mapped

📝 Post-meeting capture

Within 24 hours of any decision-heavy meeting:

  • Open a Conversation, paste the key decisions made
  • For each decision, click 💡 Save as KB fact
  • The brain now reflects today's reality, not last quarter's

🩺 Quarterly KB refresh

First Monday of each quarter:

  • Run Audit KB freshness playbook
  • Walk the proposed corrections; apply the ones you trust
  • Re-upload any document that's had a major revision

🧑‍🎓 New hire onboarding

Their first week:

  • Walk them through Refine KB → Facts in the KB by community
  • Have them run Explore conversations on the top three themes
  • By end of week, they should have added 3+ "Confirm" refinements

💡 Idea sourcing

When the roadmap needs fresh input:

  • Run Find unexplored ideas with web grounding ON
  • Pay extra attention to the wildcard chip — that's the seed of next quarter's bet
  • Send the Opportunity Scan to your design partners as a discussion prompt

🧯 Pre-mortem before committing

Any irreversible decision:

  • Run Pre-mortem on a plan playbook
  • Add a Doubt refinement on the riskiest claim it surfaces
  • Bring the artifact to the commit meeting — let dissent be visible, not hidden
FAQ & gotchas

Common questions.

Where's my answer actually coming from?
Every assistant turn carries badges that tell you the source: graph-grounded (corpus), general knowledge (LLM background), graph silent (no KB hit), web-verified (Anthropic web search), memory (your persistent memory injected). If a Refinement applies, the answer cites it inline with your attribution. Be wary of "general knowledge" answers — they're not corpus-grounded, just the LLM's prior.
How do I undo a refinement?
Open Refine KB → My refinements, find the row, click Delete. The original graph extraction resurfaces immediately. For corrections specifically, you can also use the "Restore default" button when editing. The graph itself is never mutated; refinements are a layer above it.
Why is the LLM expensive sometimes?
Most calls use Claude Haiku (cheap, fast). Playbooks default to Sonnet for the synthesis step — high quality. Inference strategies like Best-of-3 and CoVe multiply token cost 2-4x. If a Playbook run is taking long or you're worried about cost, turn off Web grounding and Fact-check on the kickoff form.
Can I edit a built-in Playbook?
Yes. Open Playbooks → Builder, click a built-in, hit Edit (override). Your edit overrides the built-in for this workspace. The original lives in code so you can Restore default any time. Same pattern works for built-in Intents, Personas, and Rubrics.
How do I keep the KB clean as our docs change?
Re-upload the document under the same name. The graph rebuilds; the diff banner in Refine KB tells you what changed (added / removed / relabeled nodes). Any refinements you wrote against removed nodes get flagged as orphaned for re-attachment.
Should the Rubric be the same for every team?
No. Workspaces are isolated and each can have its own Rubric. Sales' brain probably wants "we sell to mid-market US healthcare" framing; Engineering's brain wants "we own platform reliability, not customer empathy." That's the point.
What's the right cadence for running Playbooks?
Pre-meeting Pressure-test: 2 hours before any decision meeting.
Find unexplored ideas: once a month, or whenever the roadmap feels stale.
Audit KB freshness: once a quarter (first Monday).
ForeSight: before any commitment you can't easily reverse.
Codebase health: before any major architectural decision.
What if the LLM is just wrong?
Click 💡 save as KB fact on the wrong answer. Pick "Fix". Type the right answer. From then on, every future answer that touches the same nodes will cite your correction. The brain learns from you — that's the whole design.
One last thing. InnoBrain isn't a search engine and it isn't a chatbot. It's a shared brain. Its quality is a function of what your team puts into it — documents, refinements, memory, rubric. Treat it like a team member who needs onboarding. The first month is investment; from month two onward it pays you back.
